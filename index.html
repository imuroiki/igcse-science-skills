The two big levers
01

Power skills

If you only work on two things, work on these. Weak maths and misread command words are the most common reasons strong students lose marks at IGCSE - even when they understand the science.

√Maths fluency

Physics at IGCSE is far more numerical than Checkpoint.

  • ✓I can rearrange an equation to make any quantity the subject (e.g. turn v = s/t into t = s/v).
  • ✓I can convert units confidently (km↔m, g↔kg, cm³↔m³, minutes↔seconds).
  • ✓I can read and write numbers in standard form (0.00045 = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴).
  • ✓I can round to a sensible number of significant figures.
  • ✓I understand directly and inversely proportional - in words and on a graph.
  • ✓I can work out percentages and percentage change.
  • ✓I can estimate whether my answer is a sensible size.

?Reading command words

The exam's hidden language - answer exactly what's asked.

  • ✓I know the difference between state, describe and explain.
  • ✓I can explain WHY something happens, not just describe what happens.
  • ✓I spot the command word first and answer only what it asks.
  • ✓I understand calculate, determine, compare, suggest and define.
  • ✓I match the number of marks to the number of points I make.

02

Graphs & handling data

Plotting and reading graphs is tested in every science. A graph done carelessly throws away easy marks.

⊞Drawing graphs

  • ✓I put the thing I change (independent) on the x-axis, the thing I measure (dependent) on the y-axis.
  • ✓I choose sensible scales that fill most of the grid.
  • ✓I label both axes with the quantity AND unit.
  • ✓I plot points accurately and draw a line or curve of best fit.

∂Reading graphs

  • ✓I can calculate a gradient and say what it represents (gradient of distance–time = speed).
  • ✓I can find the area under a graph and say what it represents (area under speed–time = distance).
  • ✓I can read off intercepts and values between points.
  • ✓I can describe the shape of a graph in words.

03

Measuring & practical work

Good measurements are the foundation of good experiments. Know your instruments and know their limits.

⊢Using instruments

  • ✓I read scales correctly and avoid parallax error (eye level with the scale).
  • ✓I read a measuring cylinder from the bottom of the meniscus.
  • ✓I can use rulers, thermometers, stopwatches, balances, ammeters and voltmeters, and protractors.

≈Trusting your data

  • ✓I take repeat readings and calculate a mean.
  • ✓I know the difference between accuracy and precision.
  • ✓I know every measurement has some uncertainty (random vs systematic error).
  • ✓I can spot and ignore an anomalous result.
All sciences
04

Planning an investigation

Designing a fair test and judging it honestly is a skill examiners reward heavily - and it transfers to every subject.

⚐Designing it

  • ✓I can identify the independent, dependent and control variables.
  • ✓I can write a clear, testable prediction or hypothesis.
  • ✓I can plan a fair test (change one thing, keep the rest the same).
  • ✓I record data in a neat table with headed columns and units.

✦Judging it

  • ✓I can tell the difference between my results and my conclusion.
  • ✓I can evaluate my method: what limited it and how to improve it.
  • ✓I can decide whether my results actually support my prediction.
All sciences
05

Communicating like a scientist

The right answer written the wrong way still loses marks. These habits protect every mark you've earned.

∴Showing answers

  • ✓I always show my working - method marks survive an arithmetic slip.
  • ✓I give every answer with the correct unit.
  • ✓I quote answers to a sensible number of significant figures.

AUsing words

  • ✓I use the correct scientific terms and spell key words correctly.
  • ✓I structure a long answer logically, one point at a time.
  • ✓I write in clear cause-and-effect sentences ("because… so…").
Remember this

A physical quantity = a number × a unit.

This one idea fixes more mistakes than almost anything else. "5" is not an answer in physics - "5 metres" is. Carry your units through every calculation and your answers will tell you when something has gone wrong.

The specific ideas and equations that everything else in IGCSE Physics is built on. Get fluent with these first.

⚛Quantities & units

  • ✓I know every quantity is a number AND a unit.
  • ✓I'm confident with the main SI units (m, kg, s, A, K…).
  • ✓I know the difference between a scalar and a vector.

fFirst equations

  • ✓I can use and rearrange density ρ = m/V.
  • ✓I can use and rearrange speed v = s/t.
  • ✓I can describe energy as stored and transferred - and know it is conserved.
Quick reference
07

Command words decoded

Keep this handy. The command word tells you exactly what kind of answer earns the marks.

State / Define
Give a short fact or the meaning of a term. No explanation needed.
Describe
Say what happens or what you observe - the facts, in order.
Explain
Say WHY it happens - give the reason or mechanism. (This is the big one.)
Calculate
Work out a number. Show working and give the unit.
Determine
Work out a value using given data, a graph, or a measurement.
Compare
Give similarities AND differences - link the two things in each sentence.
Suggest
Apply what you know to a new or unfamiliar situation.
